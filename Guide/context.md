# Campus 360 — Product Context

## 1. Background

Campus 360 began as a university final-year project conceived as an online TV / digital video experience. The early UI borrowed heavily from Netflix, YouTube and short-form media patterns: Shows, Podcasts, Quickies, streaming rows, Continue Watching, account screens and vertical stories.

The project has since matured. Campus 360 has real university recognition for campus news, yet its owned digital infrastructure remains weak and much of its reach is social-media dependent.

The rebuild is therefore **not a cosmetic redesign**. It is a product repositioning and operational rebuild.

## 2. Product definition

> **The youth-first information and culture network for campus life.**

Expanded:

> Campus 360 helps students know what is happening, discover what matters, find opportunities and experience the people, stories and culture shaping university life.

Internal shorthand:

> **If it matters on campus, Campus 360 should know about it.**

The strategic territory sits between:
- official university communication;
- social media;
- conventional national news;
- student word-of-mouth;
- campus culture and entertainment.

Campus360.com becomes the owned source of truth, archive, search, depth, utility, analytics and monetization layer.

## 3. Problem being solved

Campus information is fragmented across:
- WhatsApp groups;
- TikTok;
- Instagram;
- X;
- university websites;
- student associations;
- posters;
- campus blogs;
- friends/class groups;
- social video.

Students repeatedly ask:
- What actually happened?
- Is the rumor true?
- What is happening this weekend?
- What opportunities are still open?
- What is relevant to my campus?
- What are students talking about?
- Where can I find a useful service?
- Where is the full episode I saw clipped on social?

Campus 360 should reduce that fragmentation.

## 4. Audience hierarchy

### Primary
Current university students / campus-age youth.

### Primary-adjacent
Prospective students, finalists and recent graduates.

### Supply side
Journalists, correspondents, creators, photographers, videographers, presenters and contributors.

### Institutional
Universities, student organizations, clubs, event organizers, NGOs and partners.

### Commercial
Brands, sponsors, employers, vendors, event partners.

### Operational
Editors, producers, commercial staff, management and administrators.

Critical distinction:

> **Students are primarily the audience. Brands are primarily the customer.**

## 5. Student behavioral states

- **Connected Student** — “What is happening?”
- **Fresher** — “How does this place work?”
- **Opportunity Hunter** — “What can move me forward?”
- **Culture Seeker** — “What are people watching, wearing, discussing and enjoying?”
- **Campus Citizen** — “What decisions and events affect student life?”
- **Career-Building Finalist** — “What comes after university?”
- **Creator / Contributor** — “Where can my work and voice be seen?”
- **Campus Explorer** — “Where should I go, eat, print, live or find a service?”

## 6. Core jobs-to-be-done

### Breaking
When something significant is happening around my university, I want a fast and trustworthy explanation so I know what is actually going on without digging through rumors and WhatsApp groups.

### Fresher
When I am joining university and do not understand campus life yet, I want practical and cultural guidance so I can settle in quickly.

### Opportunity
When I am trying to build my future while studying, I want relevant opportunities gathered in one trustworthy place so I do not miss internships, scholarships, jobs or programs.

### Culture
When I want to feel connected to university youth culture, I want entertaining, recognizable and shareable stories about the people and trends around me.

### Campus citizen
When a decision or event affects student life, I want understandable, impartial coverage so I can form my own view.

### Finalist
As I approach graduation, I want content and opportunities that connect university life to the professional world.

### Campus explorer
When I need something around campus, I want to quickly discover relevant nearby businesses and services.

## 10. Mobile/connectivity assumptions

Assume a user may arrive:
- on Android;
- from WhatsApp;
- using mobile data;
- with limited patience;
- on inconsistent connectivity.

Therefore:
- no autoplay 1080p homepage video;
- adaptive streaming;
- optimized responsive images;
- text-first article rendering;
- lazy media;
- conservative client JS;
- strong WhatsApp sharing;
- no mandatory login.

## 11. Editorial speed problem

The old operating pattern often waited for polished production.

New model separates **information velocity** from production polish.

A single event can generate:
1. verified Breaking Update;
2. short Article update;
3. images;
4. social clip;
5. full Article;
6. 360 News package;
7. follow-up interview;
8. Hotseat discussion;
9. archive/Collection.

Indicative newsroom targets:
- critical breaking: 10–20 min after verification;
- important update: under 60 min;
- standard news: 2–4 hours;
- social clip: under 60 min when feasible;
- recap: 6–12 hours;
- produced package: under 24 hours.

## 12. Product principles

- Information before spectacle.
- Mobile first.
- Campus context increases relevance without hiding important cross-campus stories.
- No mandatory auth.
- Accounts must unlock utility, not just profiles.
- Search is a major product surface.
- Structured entities over generic posts.
- Breaking Updates distinct from Articles.
- Events persist after occurrence.
- Opportunities auto-expire from active discovery.
- Vendors are Campus Guide listings, not marketplace sellers in V1.
- Commercial relationships are structured/disclosed.
- Media assets are reusable.
- Video is important but not dominant.
- The site is not a Complex clone.
- Internal tools are products, not generic admin panels.

## 13. Anti-goals

Do not turn Campus 360 into:
- Netflix for campus;
- YouTube clone;
- TikTok clone;
- generic social network;
- student DMs/groups platform;
- marketplace super-app;
- banner-ad farm;
- university corporate site;
- old-fashioned news portal;
- generic AI SaaS dashboard.

## 14. Flywheel

Better coverage → more student attention → higher repeat use → more contributors/sources → better campus intelligence → more valuable audience → more sponsor/vendor interest → more revenue → better journalism/production → better coverage.
